1 Introduction
1.1 Research Background
With the rapid iteration and large-scale deployment of large language model (LLM) technolo-
gies, artificial intelligence generated content (AIGC) has been widely adopted in areas such as
copywriting, knowledge dissemination, and business document composition. Text generated
by LLMs offers advantages in high efficiency and diverse expression, but it also poses serious
challenges to intellectual property (IP) protection. On one hand, the ownership of AI-generated
content is ambiguous, making infringement difficult to trace. On the other hand, attackers can
modify generated content through paraphrasing, synonym substitution, and text rewriting, fur-
ther complicating content provenance and forensic evidence collection. Against this backdrop,
watermarking techniques for LLMs have emerged as a core solution to these IP protection chal-
lenges.
However, mainstream token-level watermarking algorithms generally face a prominent ro-
bustness bottleneck: minor token-level tampering such as synonym replacement or random
deletion can cause a sharp drop in detection performance, which severely limits the practical
value of watermarking technology in real-world dissemination scenarios. How to improve the
attack resistance of watermarks while ensuring text quality and detection effectiveness has be-
come an urgent problem to be solved in current LLM watermarking research.
1.2 Technical Definition and Core Evaluation Criteria
LLM watermarking technology embeds imperceptible feature signals into the text generated
by the model or into the model weights themselves. Without compromising text readability,
it enables traceability and authorship attribution of AI-generated content, thereby providing
technical support for IP protection of LLM outputs. A practically useful LLM watermarking
algorithm must simultaneously satisfy three core performance requirements:
•Effectiveness:The watermark detection algorithm can stably and accurately identify
watermarked text.
•Imperceptibility:The watermark embedding process has minimal impact on text fluency
and semantic integrity, remaining unnoticeable to human observation.

•Robustness:After common removal attacks such as paraphrasing, rewriting, or noise
injection, the watermark signal remains detectable.
1.3 Current Research Status
The academic community and open-source community have proposed various LLM text wa-
termarking schemes. Among them, the KGW algorithm based on vocabulary probability dis-
tribution perturbation [1], and the EXP algorithm based on exponential probability bias [2],
are two representative classic approaches. Both have been engineered and made available via
GitHub open-source projects; the official implementation of the EXP algorithm is integrated
into the MarkLLM open-source toolkit [3], which is widely used for algorithm reproduction
and performance validation.
However, existing research still has two notable gaps. First, most open-source projects fo-
cus on independent code implementation, and there is a lack of horizontal comparative analysis
of the two algorithms in terms of effectiveness, imperceptibility, and robustness under unified
experimental conditions. Moreover, performance varies significantly across different runtime
environments and parameter configurations, making it difficult to assess their applicability in
real-world IP protection scenarios. Second, single watermarking algorithms have obvious struc-
tural limitations. Both KGW and EXP are typical token-level watermarks whose signals rely
on the exact identity of generated tokens. Existing studies have confirmed that both algorithms
suffer catastrophic performance degradation under simple word-level attacks such as synonym
substitution and token deletion [4]. At present, few studies have explored multi-mechanism
fusion schemes to complement the advantages of different watermark mechanisms and improve
overall attack resistance.
1.4 Work of This Paper
To address the above issues, this paper carries out the following work:
1. Fully reproduce the classic KGW and EXP LLM text watermarking algorithms based on
open-source code repositories [?,?], and conduct controlled comparative experiments un-
der a unified environment. We systematically evaluate the two algorithms around three
core metrics — effectiveness, imperceptibility, and robustness — and analyze their tech-
nical characteristics and applicable scenarios.
2. Propose a parallel fusion watermarking algorithm combining KGW and EXP mecha-
nisms. The algorithm applies KGW logit bias to green-list tokens in the pre-sampling
stage, and adopts EXP-based exponential resampling for final token selection, so as to
integrate the statistical signals of both mechanisms. In the detection stage, the Fisher
combination test is used to fuse thep-values of the two sub-detectors to realize joint
decision-making.
3. Conduct comparative experiments on the proposed fusion algorithm with the two baseline
algorithms. We verify the performance improvement of the fusion scheme in terms of
attack resistance, and evaluate its text quality and baseline detection effectiveness, so as
to verify the balance between robustness and imperceptibility of the scheme.
Our work provides a reference for the engineering deployment and future optimization of
LLM watermarking technologies.

2 Problem Definition
This chapter clarifies the research problem, experimental constraints, and quantitative evalua-
tion criteria in the context of LLM text watermark deployment. We establish a unified experi-
mental framework for both classic algorithm reproduction and fusion algorithm validation.
2.1 Research Questions
Existing studies mostly reproduce either KGW or EXP in isolation, lacking a horizontal com-
parison under identical conditions, and quantitative performance data for the three metrics are
insufficient. Meanwhile, there is a lack of exploration on multi-mechanism fusion watermarking
schemes. This paper reproduces both classic algorithms under the same environment, proposes
a KGW-EXP fusion algorithm, and conducts experiments around five specific questions:
1. Under unified conditions, what are the differences in baseline detection effectiveness be-
tween KGW and EXP, and how do key parameters affect their effectiveness?
2. Compare the text fluency after watermark embedding for both baseline algorithms, and
analyze how parameter variations affect imperceptibility.
3. Under synonym substitution and word deletion attacks, compare the robustness of the two
baseline algorithms against tampering.
4. What is the baseline detection performance of the proposed KGW-EXP fusion algorithm,
and does it achieve better attack resistance than single algorithms under the two typical
tampering scenarios?
5. Compared with the two baseline algorithms, how is the text quality of the fusion algo-
rithm, and whether it achieves a better balance between robustness and imperceptibility?
2.2 Scope Boundaries

2.3 Definition of Core Evaluation Metrics
To enable quantitative evaluation of algorithm performance, and in line with common practices
in the LLM watermarking field, we refine the three core metrics — effectiveness, impercepti-
bility, and robustness — as follows. All three algorithms are evaluated using the same metric
system.
2.3.1 Watermark Effectiveness
Effectiveness measures the algorithm’s ability to identify watermarked text. It is the foundation
for traceability and copyright determination, requiring accurate discrimination between water-
marked and non-watermarked (original) text. This experiment uses two metrics: True Positive
Rate (TPR) and separation of detection distributions.
True Positive Rate (TPR).TPR is the core quantitative metric for evaluating watermark
detection capability, representing the proportion of all watermarked samples correctly identified
as carrying a watermark:
TPR= Number of watermarked texts correctly identified

TPR ranges from 0 to 1; the closer to 1, the higher the detection accuracy. In the experiments,

Separation of Detection Distributions.This metric assesses the ability to distinguish wa-

watermark signal characteristics and better effectiveness.

3 Algorithm Design
3.1 General Principle of LLM Text Watermarking
When a large language model generates text, it scores each candidate token based on the preced-
ing context; higher scores are more likely to be selected. Probability perturbation watermarks
exploit this property as follows:
•Embedding:Using a secret key known only to the watermarker, the scores of some
tokens are artificially adjusted, guiding the model to preferentially select those tokens
and leaving an invisible statistical trace.
•Detection:The same key is used to reconstruct which tokens were “specially marked”,
and corresponding statistical tests are performed to determine whether the text contains a
watermark.
3.2 KGW Watermark Algorithm
Proposed by Kirchenbauer et al. [1], KGW is the most widely used and reproduced text water-
marking algorithm. Its core idea is “vocabulary splitting + score boosting.”
3.2.1 Basic Idea
Before generating each token, the secret key is used to randomly split all candidate tokens into
a green list and a red list. A uniform biasδis added to the scores of green-list tokens, making
the model more likely to choose them. The resulting text exhibits a statistical pattern: green-list
tokens appear more frequently. Detection exploits this pattern and uses az-test to identify the
watermark.

--- PAGE 6 ---
3.2.2 Core Procedure
Watermark Embedding:
1. Use the secret key to generate a random splitting rule based on the previous token, divid-
ing candidate tokens into green and red lists.
2. Add a fixed biasδto all green-list tokens; leave red-list token scores unchanged.
3. Apply softmax to obtain the adjusted probability distribution, and sample the next token.
4. Repeat the above steps to generate the entire text token by token.
Watermark Detection:
1. Use the same secret key to reconstruct the green-list split for each token position.
2. Count the total number of tokens in the text that belong to the green list.
3. Compute a normalizedz-score; if it exceeds the preset threshold, the text is classified as
watermarked.
3.2.3 Key Parameters
•Watermark strengthδ:The amount of bias added to green-list tokens. Increasingδ
makes the watermark easier to detect but may affect text fluency.
•Green-list ratioγ:The proportion of green-list tokens among all candidates at each split.
The original paper recommendsγ= 0.5.
•Detection threshold:The criticalz-score value for deciding whether a text is water-
marked. The original paper recommends 4.0, which yields a very low false positive rate.
3.2.4 Flowchart
3.3 EXP Watermark Algorithm
EXP is an unbiased watermarking scheme based on exponential resampling [2], whose official
implementation is integrated in the MarkLLM open-source toolkit [3]. Its core idea is “expo-
nential resampling”, which does not rely on vocabulary splitting but uses statistical hypothesis
testing for watermark detection.
3.3.1 Basic Idea
Before generating each token, the secret key is used to generate a set of random weights based
on the prefix context. The algorithm performs exponential resampling on the original probabil-
ity distribution, so that the selected tokens follow a specific statistical distribution biased by the
secret key. Detection uses a statistical hypothesis test to determine whether such a distribution
shift exists in the text.

3.3.2 Core Procedure
Watermark Embedding:
1. Use the secret key and the preceding token prefix to generate a pseudorandom seed.
2. Generate a uniform random vector based on the seed, and compute the exponential re-
sampling score for each candidate token.
3. Select the token with the highest resampling score as the next output token.
4. Repeat the above steps to generate the entire text token by token.
Watermark Detection:
1. Use the same secret key to reconstruct the random weight for each token position.
2. Compute an aggregate test statistic for the entire text based on the generated tokens.
3. Use statistical hypothesis testing to compute a significancep-value. The smaller thep-
value, the higher the probability that the text is watermarked.
3.3.3 Key Parameters
•Detection thresholdτ:The criticalp-value. If thep-value is below this threshold, the
text is classified as watermarked.
•Prefix lengthk:The number of preceding tokens used as context for the pseudorandom
seed; longer prefix provides more stable seeding.

3.4 Combined KGW-EXP Watermark Algorithm
To address the insufficient robustness of single token-level watermarks against word-level tam-
pering, this paper proposes a parallel fusion watermarking scheme. The scheme integrates the
statistical signals of KGW and EXP mechanisms through two-stage embedding and Fisher com-
bined detection, so as to improve attack resistance while maintaining acceptable text quality.
3.4.1 Design Idea
The core idea of the fusion algorithm is the parallel superposition of “logit bias + exponential
resampling”. In the generation stage, the KGW mechanism is first used to bias the logits of
green-list tokens, preliminarily shaping the probability distribution; on this basis, EXP-style
exponential resampling is performed on the adjusted distribution, so that the generated text
carries both KGW and EXP watermark signals at the same time. In the detection stage, the
two independent detection signals are fused through the Fisher combination test to improve the
detection stability under tampering attacks.

5.2 Detection Accuracy
EXP and Combined both achieve perfect detection (TPR= 1.000, FPR= 0.000) under default
no-attack conditions, while KGW reaches only TPR= 0.800. Thep-value separation shown in
Figure 4 is essentially perfect for EXP: all 20 watermarked sequences receivep-values below
10−5, while no natural sequence falls below10 −2. The exponential resampling mechanism
ensures that each generated token contributes a statistically independent signal that accumulates
multiplicatively, producing an aggregatep-value that grows exponentially with sequence length.
KGW’sz-test-based detection is inherently noisier. Thez-score is a linear statistic that
averages the green-token excess over the sequence; at 200 tokens with the default threshold of
4.0, four samples fail to exceed the detection boundary (TPR= 0.800). The score distribution in
Figure 3 shows that watermarked and naturalz-scores partially overlap, a structural limitation
of the additive mechanism at moderateδ. Raisingδto 2.0 achieves TPR= 1.000but at a
significant quality cost (PPL= 13.89), illustrating that KGW cannot simultaneously optimize
both dimensions.
The Combined method resolves this tension: by fusing the two detection signals via Fisher’s
method, it achieves TPR= 1.000without requiring an aggressiveδ. All three methods achieve
FPR= 0.000, confirming that false positive rates are well controlled across all configurations.
5.3 Text Quality (Stealth Analysis)
Table 4 and Figure 13 reveal starkly different quality profiles across the three methods. KGW
(PPL= 25.05) is the only method that raises perplexity above the natural baseline (19.63), a
direct consequence of the additive logit bias forcing generation toward the green-list subset and
away from the model’s preferred token distribution. EXP (PPL= 11.17) produces anomalously
low perplexity, reflecting its Gumbel resampling mechanism which systematically selects high-
probability tokens and results in more conservative, less diverse output — lower PPL here is
not a quality improvement but a sign of reduced lexical richness.
The Combined method achieves PPL= 17.26, almost exactly matching the natural baseline
(∆PPL=−2.37). This near-neutral quality impact arises because the KGW and EXP biases
partially cancel: KGW’s green-list pressure pushes logits upward for a fixed token subset, while

EXP’s resampling redistributes probability mass more broadly. The net effect is a generation
distribution closer to the unperturbed model output. From a stealth perspective, Combined is
the strongest of the three methods: its watermarked text is statistically indistinguishable from
natural output by a perplexity-based detector, unlike KGW (significantly higher PPL) or EXP
(suspiciously lower PPL).

5.4 Robustness
The robustness results reveal a clear performance hierarchy: Combined>EXP>KGW across
both attack types, as shown in Table 8 and Figure 12.
KGW collapses most severely under both attacks (Word-S: 0.200, Word-D: 0.120), confirm-
ing that the green-list signal — which depends on exact token identities and context hashes — is
highly fragile to any token-level perturbation. EXP performs substantially better under Word-S
(TPR= 0.860), because its resampling score is computed independently at each position and
the surviving unmodified tokens still contribute strong signal to the aggregatep-value. How-
ever, EXP degrades more under Word-D (TPR= 0.360) because its jointp-value is sensitive to
sequence length reduction.
The Combined method outperforms both across the board. Under Word-S, it achieves TPR
= 0.900, a 4-point gain over EXP alone, suggesting that the residual KGW signal for unsubsti-
tuted tokens provides a modest additional boost via the Fisher combination. The largest gain is
under Word-D: Combined achieves TPR= 0.680versus EXP’s 0.360 and KGW’s 0.120. This
32-point improvement over EXP arises from the complementary degradation profiles of the two
sub-detectors: when deletion shortens the sequence and weakens EXP’s jointp-value, the KGW
z-score — computed only over surviving tokens — can still contribute meaningful signal, and
the Fisher fusion lifts the combined statistic above the detection threshold in marginal cases
where neither method alone would succeed.

These results demonstrate that the hybrid design directly addresses the robustness limitation
of token-level watermarks. Although neither KGW nor EXP alone provides reliable protec-
tion under realistic word-level attacks, their statistical fusion substantially raises the bar for an
adversary attempting removal.
5.5 Pros, Cons, and Deployment Recommendations
Based on our experiments, we summarize the practical deployment profile of each method.
EXPis the preferred single-method choice when detection reliability and text quality un-
der benign conditions are the primary concerns. Its key advantages are: (1) perfect TPR and
FPR= 0at default settings; (2) low PPL (11.17), though this reflects reduced diversity rather
than genuine quality improvement; and (3) near-zero sensitivity to hyperparameters. Its main
limitation is vulnerability to word-level attacks, particularly Word-D (TPR= 0.360).
KGWmay be preferred where interpretability and explicit parameter control are impor-
tant. Itsδ–γparameter space provides a transparent detectability–quality trade-off. However,
it achieves the weakest robustness of the three methods, and its PPL elevation (25.05) makes
watermarked text noticeably less fluent. The catastrophic failure atγ= 0.75(TPR= 0.000) is
an important practical warning about misconfiguration risk.
Combinedis the recommended method for adversarial deployment scenarios. It achieves
perfect no-attack detection, near-baseline text quality (∆PPL=−2.37), and the strongest ro-
bustness under both Word-S (0.900) and Word-D (0.680). The main trade-off is increased con-
figuration complexity: two separate watermark configs plus Fisher fusion parameters must be
maintained. For IP protection use cases where paraphrasing attacks are a realistic threat, Com-
bined’s robustness advantage decisively outweighs this overhead.
6 Conclusion
In this paper, we reproduced and systematically evaluated two representative LLM watermark-
ing methods — KGW and EXP — and proposed a Combined hybrid method that fuses both via
Fisher’s statistical combination. Experiments were conducted on text generated by Qwen2.5-
1.5B-Instruct using the MarkLLM toolkit, covering three core desiderata: detection accuracy
(TPR and FPR), text quality (PPL evaluated by GPT-2), and robustness against word-level para-
phrasing attacks.
Our experiments yield four principal findings. First, under benign conditions EXP is strictly
superior to KGW as a standalone method: EXP achieves perfect detection (TPR= 1.000,
FPR= 0.000) with a watermarked PPL of 11.17, versus KGW’s TPR= 0.800and PPL=
25.05. KGW’s interpretable green-list mechanism comes at a real cost — its detection reliability
depends heavily on the choice ofδandγ, and misconfiguration (e.g.,γ= 0.75) causes complete
detection failure.
Second, both KGW and EXP are highly vulnerable to word-level attacks when used alone.
Under Word-S (20% substitution), KGW drops to TPR= 0.200and EXP to 0.860; under
Word-D (30% deletion), KGW drops to 0.120 and EXP to 0.360. This exposes a fundamen-
tal limitation of generation-time token-level watermarks: the embedded signal depends on the
exact identity of generated tokens, and any operation that alters token identities — even while
preserving meaning — degrades detectability.
Third, the proposed Combined method substantially addresses this robustness limitation. By
jointly embedding KGW and EXP signals during generation and fusing their detection statis-
tics via Fisher’s method, Combined achieves TPR= 1.000with no attack, TPR= 0.900under

Word-S, and TPR= 0.680under Word-D — outperforming EXP by 4 and 32 percentage points
respectively under the two attacks. The Fisher fusion exploits the complementary degradation
profiles of the two sub-detectors: when synonym substitution weakens KGW’s context-hash-
based signal, EXP’s position-independent resampling score compensates; when deletion short-
ens the sequence and weakens EXP’s jointp-value, the KGWz-score over surviving tokens
provides residual uplift.
Fourth, Combined achieves near-baseline text quality (PPL= 17.26,∆PPL=−2.37rela-
tive to the natural baseline of 19.63), making it the most stealthy of the three methods. KGW’s
additive logit bias raises PPL noticeably above baseline, while EXP’s resampling anomalously
suppresses PPL below it — both deviations are potentially detectable by a perplexity-based ad-
versary. Combined’s near-neutral quality impact makes its watermarked text statistically closest
to natural LLM output.
From this project, we learned that the central challenge in LLM watermarking is notde-
tectionunder ideal conditions — all three methods achieve zero false positives, and both EXP
and Combined achieve perfect TPR without attack — butrobustnessunder adversarial pertur-
bation. A watermark that is easily removed by a basic synonym substitution tool offers limited
practical value for IP protection. The Combined method demonstrates that statistical fusion
of complementary token-level signals can meaningfully raise the robustness bar without sacri-
ficing detection accuracy or text quality. Nevertheless, even Combined’s TPR of 0.680 under
Word-D leaves room for improvement, and future work should explore semantic-level water-
marking approaches that encode signals in higher-level properties of text inherently preserved
under meaning-preserving transformations.
